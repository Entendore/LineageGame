A study in artificial selection

Lineage.

Every wild specimen carries hidden alleles — recessive traits waiting generations to surface. It falls to you, keeper of this station, to pair and hatch until the line produces the specimen the Society requests.

1Click two specimens to form a pair — or spread the examination tray (T) to compare the whole enclosure at a glance, pit any two side by side in the comparator, and sketch a vector keepsake. Arrow keys cycle specimens; Enter selects. Right-click or press-and-hold for quick actions.
2A pairing lays a clutch of one to three eggs — turn the eggs as they incubate. Kinship invites mutations and frailty; outcrossing brings vigour, or arrange an exchange with the sister station.
3Steer the bloodline toward the Society's request — ten traits in a single body. The forecast gives the exact odds, and names the genes that block a pairing entirely.
4Commissions, collector letters and quarterly exhibitions fill the ledger; grant marks buy provisions, extensions, grafts — and vitrine space, to freeze a precious specimen against time itself.
5Seasons turn, rain passes, night falls. Scatter seed on bare ground; the young scurry after their elders, learning the enclosure.
Six founders and a modest purse — the standard study.

The Society — correspondence

Letters.

Collectors pay grant marks for living specimens that answer a letter. A specimen delivered departs for good — pinned and target specimens are refused.
The Society — posted commissions

Commissions.

Side requests from the Society — fulfil them before they lapse for grant marks. New ones arrive every few days.
The sister station — exchange programme

The exchange.

Your specimen departs at once; the courier returns in a few days with a sister-line offspring — one strand from your departed line, one from theirs. Kinship is recorded either way.
